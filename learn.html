2. The NOVA Classification Framework

Peer-reviewed food processing tiers developed by researchers at the University of São Paulo.

BiteLens’s Health Processing Score (0–100) utilizes the internationally recognized NOVA Food Classification System (Monteiro et al.). NOVA evaluates foods by the extent and purpose of industrial processing rather than isolated calories:

NOVA Group 1: Unprocessed

Fresh fruits, raw grains, vegetables, whole pulses, fresh milk, and eggs. (Baseline: 100/100)

NOVA Group 2: Culinary Ingredients

Oils, butter, honey, sugar, and salt used to prepare home-cooked meals. (Baseline: 90/100)

NOVA Group 3: Processed Foods

Canned vegetables, artisanal breads, salted nuts, and cheese. (Baseline: 75/100)

NOVA Group 4: Ultra-Processed (UPFs)

Industrial formulations with emulsifiers, synthetic dyes, artificial sweeteners, and extruded starches. (Baseline: 50/100)

3. Regulatory Landscape & FSSAI Standards

Independent Heuristic Notice

India does not currently enforce a mandatory front-of-pack nutrition rating (FOPL) label. While the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has published draft guidelines, packaging labels rely on cryptic International Numbering System (INS) codes. BiteLens bridges this regulatory information gap by decoding these numbers directly into everyday plain English.

4. Decoupled Dual-Scoring Philosophy

Most legacy apps mix calories with processing into an opaque single score. BiteLens explicitly separates these into two independent dimensions:

  • Health Processing Score (0–100): Objective, population-wide measure of industrial chemical processing based on NOVA and additive risks.
  • Personal Goal Fit Score (0–100): Personalized alignment with individual dietary requirements (e.g. caloric deficit for fat loss vs. high protein density for muscle hypertrophy).
