Labeling FAQ & Myth Buster

Clearing up misconceptions about INS codes, FSSAI regulations, and processing levels in Indian packaged foods.

What is an INS number, and is it identical to a European E-number?

Yes. INS stands for the International Numbering System for Food Additives, established by Codex Alimentarius. India’s FSSAI adopts INS numbers directly. European "E-numbers" share the exact same numerical index (e.g. INS 322 is identical to E322 for lecithin).

Why can a high-protein snack bar have a low Health Score?

This is BiteLens’s Dual Scoring Principle in action. A protein bar might meet your macro goal (high Goal Fit Score), but if it relies heavily on artificial sweeteners (INS 955), emulsifiers (INS 471), and industrial soy isolates, its processing quality under the NOVA framework places it in Group 4 (lower Health Score). BiteLens presents both scores side-by-side so trade-offs are transparent.

Does India currently have an official front-of-pack star rating system?

No. While FSSAI has published draft proposals (such as Indian Nutrition Rating / INR star system concepts), no mandatory front-of-pack rating scale has been enacted into law. BiteLens’s Health Score is an independent academic heuristic—not an official government rating.

What is the difference between "Total Sugars" and "Added Sugars" on Indian labels?

Total Sugars include naturally occurring sugars present in ingredients (like lactose in milk or fructose in fruit pulp). Added Sugars refer to refined sucrose, high-fructose corn syrup, or concentrated syrups added during manufacturing. FSSAI mandates disclosing added sugars separately.

How are additive flags determined in BiteLens?

Every additive flag traces directly back to published regulatory maximum limits (FSSAI Food Safety and Standards Regulations) or peer-reviewed international food science guidelines. BiteLens never generates unsourced or arbitrary warning flags.
